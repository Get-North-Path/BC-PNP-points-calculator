# What is the BC PNP calculator?

**GetNorthPath BC PNP** is a free public calculator and guide set for British Columbia’s Skills Immigration program. The live site is [bcpnp.getnorthpath.com](https://bcpnp.getnorthpath.com/). The product name on the page is **BC PNP Category Classifier & SIRS Score Calculator**.

This GitHub repository is the **public information hub** for that product. It documents features, pages, the 200-point grid the calculator uses, the Care / Build / Innovate categories, and the official sources those pages cite. It is **not** the application source-code repository and does not contain implementation, APIs, credentials, or private infrastructure.

---

## What the calculator is

A **free DIY estimate** for people who need to know which 2026 Skills Immigration category they are in, and what SIRS score that profile produces on the grid the site encodes:

- Classify the occupation into **Care**, **Build**, **Innovate** (score route or wage route), the **rural and remote health** initiative, or **not currently on a priority list**
- Score wage, directly related experience, education, CLB language, and region out of **200**
- Compare the estimate with the **latest published minimum** for that category
- Read guides, occupation examples, and region pages that use the same figures
- Optionally request a consultation with the category and score attached

The site states the grid has been in force since **4 December 2025**, and that categories were restructured on **23 April 2026**. Age is not a scoring factor.

## What the product states it is not

From the live footer and methodology page:

- Not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to the Government of British Columbia, WelcomeBC, or IRCC
- Not an official SIRS score or category placement
- Not a guarantee of eligibility or an invitation
- Not legal, immigration, or financial advice
- Not a calculator for **Entrepreneur Immigration** or the **Health Authority** stream (both are called out of scope on the methodology page)

The footer tells readers to consult a licensed Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultant (RCIC) or an immigration lawyer for advice on their own application.
